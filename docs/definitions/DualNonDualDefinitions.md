1. Formal Dual Algebra (A6)
Formal dual algebra refers to the continuous, monotone, adjacency‑preserving
operator regime used in A6 systems. It includes:

- spectral continuity  
- modal adjacency  
- bounded holonomy drift  
- collapse‑surface stability  
- stability‑envelope monotonicity  
- unified kernel invariants  

A6 dual algebra is defined by:

- continuity  
- monotonicity  
- adjacency preservation  
- spectral boundedness  
- holonomy boundedness  

This is the operator regime that A6 requires for safety and invariant
propagation.

2. Formal Non‑Dual Algebra (A7)
Formal non‑dual algebra refers to the discontinuous, non‑monotone, adjacency‑

- divergence invariants  
- divergence membranes  
- divergence collapse surfaces  
- divergence stability envelopes  
- spectral tearing  
- modal fracture  
- holonomy accumulation  

A7 non‑dual algebra is defined by:

- discontinuity  
- non‑monotonicity  
- adjacency fracture  
- spectral tearing  
- holonomy accumulation  

This regime cannot exist inside A6 and must be bounded by the A7 divergence
membrane.

3. Structural Non‑Duality (A6 + A7)
Structural non‑duality is a design principle that applies to both A6 and A7.
It means:

- no primal/dual split  
- no mirrored operator spaces  
- no adjoint geometry  
- no dual kernels  
- no dual envelopes  
- no two‑space ontology  

This is a philosophical and architectural constraint, not an algebraic one.

A6 uses structural non‑duality but not formal non‑dual algebra.  
A7 uses both structural non‑duality and formal non‑dual algebra.
